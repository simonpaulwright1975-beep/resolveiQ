What the numbers mean

Every figure on this screen is counted from the cases you close. None of them are targets set by a machine, and none of them are guesses — if a number cannot be worked out honestly it shows a dash rather than a zero.

Open tickets

Cases that are not resolved yet — new, open and pending together.

How to meet it: this one is meant to go down through the day, not to be zero. The number underneath it, need attention, is the one worth watching: those are cases that have breached their window or are about to.

Resolved today

Cases you closed since midnight, compared with the same count yesterday.

How to meet it: resolve a case when it is genuinely finished, not to move the count. Reopening is free and costs nothing but a click — closing something that comes straight back costs the customer another wait.

"Today" is a UK day, so a case you finish at half past eleven at night still counts against that day and not the next.

AI-assisted

The share of today's resolved cases where you used the drafted reply.

How to meet it: you don't. This one is not a target and there is no right number. It is here to show whether the drafting is actually earning its place. If it stays low, the drafts are not good enough — that is worth saying, and it is a problem with the app, not with you.

Nothing is resolved automatically. Every case on this screen is closed by a person.

Average handling time

Mean minutes from a case being raised to you resolving it, across everything you closed today.

How to meet it: don't chase it. A long average usually means cases sat waiting, not that anyone worked slowly — and a case that needs a supplier to come back will always be long. Raising the case as soon as the call ends does more for this figure than working faster does.

CSAT

Customer satisfaction — the average score out of five, from customers who rated a case you closed today.

How to meet it: ask. Open a case and there is a Ask for a rating box with a link in it; paste that at the end of your reply. The customer taps a number, it lands on that case, and the average appears here.

Nobody is chasing a number. A rating can only be given once per case, links stop working after a month, and a case nobody rates simply does not count towards the average — a quiet day shows a dash rather than a bad score.

Cost of failure

What a case cost the business, if anything — a re-delivery, a credit note, goodwill, scrapped stock.

How to meet it: a case cannot be closed until you have either put a figure and a reason against it, or ticked No cost. That is not a judgement on how you handled it — most cases cost nothing, and saying so takes one tap.

You can change it afterwards. If the credit note lands three days later, open the case and use Change this — the case stays closed.

SLA compliance

The share of open cases still inside their time window. Each case gets a window from its priority:

    How to meet it: work the top of the queue. It is already sorted by urgency — breached first, then at risk, then longest waiting — so the case that needs you most is always the one at the top. If something cannot be finished inside its window, moving it to pending is honest; leaving it open and silent is what breaches look like.

    If a figure here looks wrong, it probably is — say so rather than working around it. A number nobody trusts is worse than no number at all.
